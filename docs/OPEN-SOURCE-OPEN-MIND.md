# The Open-Source Open-Mind Covenant

**A non-binding, good-faith companion to Guillotine's license — not a license itself.**

Guillotine is free and open-source software under the **GNU AGPL-3.0** (see [`LICENSE`](../LICENSE) and
[`NOTICE`](../NOTICE)). This covenant is **not** a license and **not** a condition of one. It is a request,
made in good faith and taken on good faith. Nothing here limits, adds to, or terminates any right the AGPL
grants you, and not honoring it costs you nothing. Because it binds nothing, it does **not** affect
Guillotine's open-source status.

---

## Open source, open mind

Guillotine is open so that it may **drive invention**: use it, study it, change it, build new things with
it — for any purpose, including making money.

In return we ask one thing — not as a rule, but as a courtesy between makers: **keep an open mind to the
real source.** The person who really made this keeps a standing wish to be *heard* about where it goes.
Not to command. Not to veto. Not even to be answered. Just, once, to be heard.

## The request

If You build on this work, and the original Author sends You a written statement about it, we ask that You
**cause that statement to be presented, in full, once, at the next meeting of Your board or equivalent
directional body** — if You hold such meetings.

That is the whole of it. You need not notify the Author, invite the statement, maintain any channel,
reply, agree, or act on it. One statement, read once, heard once. Whether you honor this is left entirely
to your good faith.

## Why it's only a wish

We looked hard at whether this could be a binding term. Honestly, it's arguable: worded as it is — no duty
to notify the Author or stay reachable, triggered only if a statement happens to arrive, performed once —
it sits close to the old BSD "advertising clause," which is considered free software. But *arguable* isn't
*certain*, and formal open-source approval of a novel term like this is a coin-flip. Leaving it non-binding
removes every doubt at no real cost: a good-faith request is no condition at all, so the AGPL freedoms stay
pristine and Guillotine stays unambiguously open source. The enforceable parts — open source, attribution,
the reserved name — live in the license and the `NOTICE`. This part, the *voice*, is left to honor on
purpose. It rides with the work as a hope, not a hook.

If you're reading this, you're the kind of person who reads the covenant. That's who it was written for.
